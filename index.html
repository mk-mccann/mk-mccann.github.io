About Me

I'm a neuroscientist and engineer who likes building the tools that make hard experiments possible. I trained in biomedical engineering at Duke, then spent eight years at two Max Planck institutes, finishing a PhD in neurobiology at the Max Planck Institute for Biological Intelligence in Munich.

For my PhD, I built a real-time virtual reality system for freely moving mice that tracked an animal's head at 360 frames per second and redrew its world in under 20 milliseconds. I then used machine learning to ask how the visual cortex combines what an animal sees with how it moves. That work took me across the full data stack, from electronics and motion capture through computer vision, statistical modeling, and terabyte-scale analysis pipelines.

Today I work with research teams as an independent consultant through DeepBio Analytics. I'm based in Boston, Massachusetts, and work with labs and companies everywhere, remotely or in person. I look at challenges from first principles to find solutions that work in practice. If you're stuck on a technical problem or want to think an idea through, I'd love to hear from you.
